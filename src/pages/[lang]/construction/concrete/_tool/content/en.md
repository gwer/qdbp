## How to use

Enter the length, width and thickness of a slab, patio, sidewalk or footing, and add extra for spills and an uneven base. Then there are two ways to buy:

- **bagged premix** (like Quikrete or Sakrete): enter the bag size — 60 or 80 lb — to see how many bags you need;
- **mixing your own**: pick the mix ratio and cement bag size to see the cement, sand, gravel and water.

## How it's calculated

1. Volume = length × width × thickness × (1 + extra). Concrete is ordered in cubic yards: 1 cu yd = 27 cu ft.
2. An 80-lb bag of premix makes about 0.60 cu ft of concrete and a 60-lb bag about 0.45 cu ft, so one cubic yard takes about **45 bags of 80 lb** or **60 bags of 60 lb**.
3. For a site mix, you need about 1.54 times the finished volume in dry materials — water and fines fill the gaps and the mix compacts. That's the standard factor for mixes by volume. The dry volume is split by the ratio; cement is converted to pounds at a bulk density of about 90 lb/cu ft (1440 kg/m³).
4. Water is a guide for a water-cement ratio of 0.5. Add it gradually until you reach the workability you need.

## Which mix to choose

Ratios are by volume: cement : sand : gravel (for example, in buckets).

- 1 : 2 : 3 — a stronger mix for slabs and footings that carry loads.
- 1 : 2 : 4 — a general-purpose mix for small foundations, walkways and post footings.
- 1 : 3 : 5 and 1 : 3 : 6 — lean concrete for mud slabs, fill and leveling.

These are guides, not a guaranteed strength: that depends on the cement, the aggregate, the water and compaction. A 3,000 or 4,000 psi mix can't be guaranteed from the ratio alone — for structural work, follow your plans or order ready-mix at the strength you need. Portland cement in the US comes in **94-lb bags**, which is the default here.

## Examples

- A 20 × 12 ft slab, 4 in thick, 5% extra: **84 cu ft = 3.11 cu yd**. In premix that's **140 bags of 80 lb** (or 187 bags of 60 lb) — at this size, ready-mix delivered by the truck is usually easier. Mixing 1 : 2 : 4 yourself: about **18 bags of 94-lb cement**, 1.37 cu yd of sand, 2.74 cu yd of gravel and about 100 gallons of water.
- A 10 × 10 ft patio, 4 in thick, 10% extra: 1.36 cu yd — **62 bags of 80 lb**.

## FAQ

**How much extra should I add?** 5–10%: some concrete stays in the mixer and on tools, and the subgrade is rarely perfectly flat.

**When is ready-mix worth it?** For about a cubic yard or more. Many suppliers have a minimum order, and short-load fees apply below it.

**Sand and gravel are sold by the ton — how do I convert?** Roughly 1.3–1.4 tons per cubic yard for dry sand and about 1.4 tons for gravel. Check with your supplier.
