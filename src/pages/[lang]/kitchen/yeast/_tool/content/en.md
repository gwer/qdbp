## How to use

Enter how much yeast the recipe calls for and which kind — you'll see the equivalent amount of fresh (cake) yeast, active dry yeast and instant yeast.

## How it's calculated

By weight, the ratio **fresh : active dry : instant = 1 : 0.4 : 0.33** is used — the multipliers professional bakers use (for example, Jeffrey Hamelman in *Bread*). An easy rule of thumb: you need about a third as much instant yeast as fresh.

Treat it as a guide: yeast strength varies by brand, and some charts use 0.5 for active dry.

## Examples

- One packet (¼ oz, 7 g) of active dry = **17.5 g** fresh = **5.8 g** instant.
- 7 g of instant = **21 g** fresh = **8.4 g** active dry.
- 0.6 oz (17 g) cake of fresh yeast = **5.7 g** instant.

## FAQ

**What's the difference between active dry and instant?** Active dry is usually dissolved in warm water first; instant (rapid-rise, bread machine) yeast can go straight into the flour.

**How many teaspoons is a packet?** A ¼-oz packet holds about 2¼ teaspoons. For accuracy, weigh it.

**Will rising times change?** Slightly — go by how the dough looks, not just the clock.
