## How to use

Enter the floor or wall area, the tile size and thickness, the grout joint width and the waste allowance. Add how many tiles come in a box and the thinset usage. You'll see how many tiles and boxes to buy, plus estimates for grout and thinset.

## How it's calculated

- Each tile plus its joint takes up (length + joint) × (width + joint). Tiles = area ÷ that × (1 + waste), rounded up.
- Boxes = tiles ÷ tiles per box, rounded up.
- Grout, using the formula grout manufacturers publish: usage per area = (A + B) ÷ (A × B) × tile thickness × joint width × 1.6, with A and B the tile length and width in millimeters.
- Thinset = area × thinset usage.

## How much waste to allow

- 5–10% for a straight lay with plain tile.
- 10–15% for diagonal or herringbone patterns, large-format tile and rooms with lots of cuts.
- Keep a few spare tiles for future repairs — the same dye lot is hard to find years later.

## Thinset usage

It depends on the trowel notch and is printed on the bag. As a rough guide, a 50-lb bag covers about 90–100 sq ft with a 1/4 × 1/4 in square-notch trowel (about 0.5 lb/sq ft) and about 40–50 sq ft with a 1/4 × 3/8 in or 1/2 in notch for large tile (about 1–1.25 lb/sq ft). The default is 1 lb/sq ft.

## Examples

- 100 sq ft of floor, 24 × 12 in tile, 3/8 in thick, 1/8 in joints, 10% waste: **55 tiles**, **7 boxes** of 8, about **4.9 lb of grout** and 100 lb of thinset (two 50-lb bags).
- 60 sq ft of 12 × 12 in tile, 5/16 in thick, 1/8 in joints, 10% waste, 10 per box: 65 tiles, 7 boxes, about 3.3 lb of grout.

## FAQ

**The box already lists square feet — why count tiles?** To double-check: at 16 sq ft per box and 55 tiles needed, it's still 7 boxes.

**Why so little grout?** Narrow joints with large tile really don't need much. Buy the smallest bag that covers it, with a bit to spare.
