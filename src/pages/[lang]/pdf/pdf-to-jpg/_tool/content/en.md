## How to use

Choose a PDF and thumbnails of its pages appear. Then set:

- **Format** — JPG suits photos, scans and sending in messengers. PNG is for diagrams, tables and text: it's lossless, so letter edges stay crisp, but the files are larger.
- **Resolution** — 72 dpi is enough to view on screen or put in a presentation. 150 dpi is the all-round choice. 300 dpi is for printing and small print. A Letter page at 150 dpi is 1,275 × 1,650 pixels, at 300 dpi 2,550 × 3,300 (A4: 1,240 × 1,754 and 2,480 × 3,508).
- **Pages** — an empty field means all pages. You can enter individual pages and ranges: `1-3, 7`. Pages that won't be saved are faded on the thumbnails.

Click “Save” and download the images one by one or all at once as a ZIP. File names include the page number.

## How it works

Each page is rendered in your browser by the same engine that's built into Firefox (pdf.js) and saved as an image. The file is never uploaded. A password-protected PDF opens after you enter the password.

The image size in pixels equals the page size in inches times the resolution. Very large pages, such as A1 drawings at 300 dpi, exceed the limit that phone browsers allow for a single image (about 16.7 megapixels). Such pages are saved at a lower resolution, and a note next to the file says so.

## FAQ

**Can I get the original photos out of a PDF instead of page snapshots?** This tool saves the whole page as it looks. If a photo fills the page, as in a scan, the result is practically the same. To get a photo without margins, choose a higher resolution and crop the image.

**Why is the text in the image blurry?** The resolution is most likely too low for small print. Choose 300 dpi and PNG.

**How do I do the opposite — make a PDF from images?** Use the [JPG to PDF converter](/en/pdf/jpg-to-pdf/).

**Need only the text from a PDF?** You can [extract it from the PDF](/en/pdf/pdf-to-text/) as plain text, without images.
