## How to use

Choose what you know: the time you need to wake up or the time you're going to bed. The calculator suggests a bedtime or alarm time so that you wake at the end of a sleep cycle rather than in the middle of one.

## How it's calculated

Sleep runs in cycles of roughly **90 minutes**: light sleep, deep sleep, then REM (dreaming) sleep. Waking at the end of a cycle usually feels easier than being pulled out of deep sleep — less grogginess.

`bedtime = wake-up time − cycles × 90 min − time to fall asleep`

Example: you need to be up at 7:00 AM and take 15 minutes to fall asleep. For 5 cycles (7.5 hours) go to bed at **11:15 PM**; for 6 cycles (9 hours), at 9:45 PM.

## FAQ

**How many cycles do I need?** Adults are advised to get 7–9 hours — that's 5 or 6 cycles. Four cycles (6 hours) is a one-off fallback; three is for emergencies only.

**Is a sleep cycle always 90 minutes?** No, that's an average: cycles range from about 70 to 120 minutes and vary through the night. If waking is still hard, try moving your alarm 10–15 minutes either way.

**Why include time to fall asleep?** Cycles start when you're asleep, not when you lie down. Healthy adults usually fall asleep within 10–20 minutes.

If sleep problems last for weeks, talk to a doctor.
