## How to use

Enter your weight, how long you exercise each day and whether it's hot outside. You'll see roughly how much water to drink a day, in ounces or liters and in cups.

## How it's calculated

- **30 mL per kilogram of body weight** (about ½ oz per pound) — the baseline;
- **+12 oz (350 mL) for every 30 minutes of exercise** — to replace sweat;
- **+17 oz (0.5 L) in hot weather**.

For example, at 160 lb with 30 minutes of exercise: about **85 oz** — roughly 11 cups.

## FAQ

**Do tea, soup and fruit count?** Yes. Your body gets water from all drinks and food — about a fifth comes from food. The result is what to drink, but there's no need to count every glass.

**Do I really need 8 glasses a day?** It's a popular rule of thumb without solid science behind it. The U.S. National Academies suggest about 2.7 L (91 oz) of total water a day for women and 3.7 L (125 oz) for men, including food.

**Can you drink too much water?** Yes, though it's rare: several liters in a couple of hours can dangerously lower blood sodium. Spread your drinking through the day and let thirst guide you.

This is a guide for healthy adults. With kidney or heart conditions, or during pregnancy and breastfeeding, ask your doctor how much to drink.
