## How to use

Enter the first day of your last menstrual period and your usual cycle length. You'll see your estimated due date, how many weeks and days pregnant you are, your trimester and an estimated conception date. You can also check how far along you'll be on any other date.

## How it's calculated

It uses Naegele's rule: **280 days** (40 weeks) are added to the first day of your last period. If your cycle is longer or shorter than 28 days, the date shifts by the difference. Pregnancy is counted in gestational weeks — from the first day of your last period, not from conception.

## Example

Last period started on March 1, 2026, with a 28-day cycle:

- estimated due date: **December 6, 2026**;
- on May 10, 2026 you're **10 weeks** pregnant, in the first trimester;
- estimated conception: around March 15.

With a 32-day cycle, the due date moves to December 10.

## FAQ

**How accurate is the due date?** Only about 4–5% of babies are born on their due date; it's an estimate, not a prediction of the day of birth. Your maternity care team determines pregnancy dating and follow-up; first-trimester ultrasound dating is more accurate and may differ from a calculation based on your last period.

**How are gestational weeks different from weeks since conception?** Gestational age is about 2 weeks more, because it's counted from the start of your last cycle.

**What if I don't remember the date of my last period?** Your doctor or midwife can date the pregnancy with an ultrasound.

This calculator is for information only and doesn't replace prenatal care.
