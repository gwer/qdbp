## How to use

Choose your sex and enter your weight, height and tape-measure readings. You'll see your body fat percentage by the U.S. Navy method, the category it falls into and how much of your weight is fat versus lean mass.

## How to measure

- **Neck** — just below the Adam's apple, with the tape sloping slightly down at the front.
- **Waist** — men at the navel, women at the narrowest point. Don't suck in; measure after breathing out.
- **Hips** (women only) — around the widest part of the buttocks.

Keep the tape snug but not tight. Measure two or three times and use the average.

## Formula

The method was developed at the Naval Health Research Center (Hodgdon and Beckett, 1984) and is still used for military fitness checks. In inches:

- Men: `body fat % = 86.010 × log10(waist − neck) − 70.041 × log10(height) + 36.76`
- Women: `body fat % = 163.205 × log10(waist + hip − neck) − 97.684 × log10(height) − 78.387`

A 5′10″ man with a 15″ neck and 34″ waist comes out at about **17.5%**.

## Body fat ranges (ACE)

| Category | Men | Women |
|---|---|---|
| Essential fat | 2–5% | 10–13% |
| Athletes | 6–13% | 14–20% |
| Fitness | 14–17% | 21–24% |
| Average | 18–24% | 25–31% |
| Obese | 25%+ | 32%+ |

Ranges from the American Council on Exercise.

## FAQ

**How accurate is it?** Typically within 3–4 percentage points of lab methods such as DEXA. That's good enough to track progress — just measure the same way at the same time of day.

**Why is body fat better than BMI?** [BMI](/en/health/bmi/) can't tell muscle from fat, while this method relies on girths that grow mostly with fat. A muscular athlete with an "overweight" BMI can have perfectly healthy body fat.

The result is an estimate, not a diagnosis.
