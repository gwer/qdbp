## How to use

Enter text, choose a shift and whether to encrypt or decrypt. Each letter is replaced by the letter a set number of places further along the alphabet (or back, when decrypting). Numbers, spaces and punctuation stay as they are, and upper and lower case are preserved.

## Examples

- Hello, World! with shift 3 → **Khoor, Zruog!**
- Shift 13 is **ROT13**: applying it twice gives back the original text, which is why it's used to hide spoilers.

## FAQ

**How secure is the Caesar cipher?** Not at all: there are only 25 possible shifts, so anyone can try them by hand. It's a teaching and puzzle cipher — great for escape rooms and classrooms. For real protection, use modern encryption and [strong passwords](/en/security/password-generator/).

**How do I decrypt without knowing the shift?** Switch to "Decrypt" and slide through the shifts — readable text will appear at one of them.

**Why did Julius Caesar use a shift of 3?** According to the historian Suetonius, Caesar replaced each letter with the one three places after it.
