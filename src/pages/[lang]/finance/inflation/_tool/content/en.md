## How to use

Enter an amount, the expected annual inflation rate and the number of years. You'll see two things: what something that costs this amount today will cost in the future, and what the same amount of money will be worth in today's dollars.

## Formulas

- **Future cost** = amount × (1 + inflation)^years.
- **Purchasing power** = amount ÷ (1 + inflation)^years.
- **Purchasing power lost** = 1 − 1 ÷ (1 + inflation)^years.

## Example

At 3% inflation, something that costs $1,000 today will cost **$1,343.92** in 10 years. And $1,000 kept in cash will buy only what $744.09 buys today — a **25.59%** loss of purchasing power.

## FAQ

**What inflation rate should I use?** The Federal Reserve targets 2% over the long run; historical CPI averages have been higher. Treat any rate as an assumption — actual inflation varies from year to year.

**How do I compare this with my savings?** If your savings earn less than inflation, their real value shrinks. Model growth with the [compound interest calculator](/en/finance/compound-interest/), or check an investment's return with the [ROI calculator](/en/finance/roi/).

**Why does my inflation feel different?** CPI tracks a broad basket of goods and services; your personal rate depends on what you actually buy.

The calculation assumes a constant inflation rate and is for reference only.
