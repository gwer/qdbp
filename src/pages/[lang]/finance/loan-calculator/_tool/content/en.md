## How to use

Enter the loan amount, the annual interest rate and the term in months, then choose the repayment type. You'll instantly see the monthly payment, total interest and total amount paid, plus a month-by-month amortization schedule.

## Fixed payments vs. equal principal

**Fixed payments (amortizing loan)** — every payment is the same. Early on most of it goes to interest; towards the end, most goes to principal. Most personal loans, car loans and mortgages work this way.

`payment = P × r / (1 − (1 + r)^−n)`, where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly rate (annual rate / 12 / 100) and n is the number of months.

**Equal principal** — you repay the same amount of principal every month, and interest is charged on the remaining balance, so each payment is smaller than the last. Payments start higher, but you pay less interest overall.

## Example

A $25,000 loan at 7% APR for 5 years (60 months), fixed payments: about **$495.03** a month and roughly **$4,702** in total interest.

## FAQ

**Why is the last payment slightly different?** Payments are rounded to the cent, so the final payment clears whatever balance is left — the same way lenders do it.

**Are fees and insurance included?** No, the calculation covers principal and interest only. Check your loan's APR disclosure for the full cost.

**Will it match my lender's numbers?** Usually to the cent. Some lenders charge interest by the actual number of days in each month, which can cause small differences.
