## How to use

Enter the cost and the selling price — you'll see the profit margin, the profit and the markup.

For the reverse question — what price gives, say, a 30% margin — choose “Price for a target margin” in the “Find” field.

## Formulas

- `profit = price − cost`
- `margin = profit / price × 100`
- `markup = profit / cost × 100`

Cost $800, price $1,000: profit $200, margin **20%**, markup **25%**.

## Margin vs. markup

Margin is based on the selling price and markup on the cost, so for the same profit the markup is always higher. A 20% margin equals a 25% markup, 30% equals 42.86%, and 50% equals 100%.

## Examples

- Cost $120, price $100 — a loss: profit −$20, margin −20%.
- To get a 30% margin on a cost of $800: price = 800 / (1 − 0.3) = **$1,142.86**.

## FAQ

**Is this gross margin?** Yes: the difference between revenue and the cost of goods sold, as a percentage of revenue. Operating expenses and taxes aren't included.

**How do I set a price from a markup?** Use the [markup calculator](/en/finance/markup/).
