## How to use

Enter the loan amount, interest rate, full term in months, how many payments you've already made and the size of your extra payment. You'll see the interest saved for the option you choose, and the table below compares both options side by side: pay the loan off sooner, or lower the monthly payment.

The calculation is for a fixed-payment (amortizing) loan and a one-time lump-sum extra payment. Amounts are rounded to the cent each month, as on a lender's amortization schedule.

## Shorter term or lower payment?

If you **shorten the term**, your payment stays the same and the loan is paid off sooner. If you **lower the payment** (lenders often call it recasting), the term stays the same and the monthly payment drops. Shortening the term almost always saves more interest, because the same payment keeps going mostly to principal.

## Example

A $300,000 mortgage at 7% for 30 years has a payment of $1,995.91. After 60 payments, you make a one-time extra payment of $20,000:

- **shorter term** — 251 payments left instead of 300 (paid off 4 years 1 month sooner), saving **$79,059.96**;
- **lower payment** — new payment $1,854.55, saving **$22,404.46**.

## FAQ

**Is there a prepayment penalty?** Most US mortgages today don't have one, and federal rules limit them on many loans. Some loans do — check your loan documents or ask your servicer before paying extra.

**When is prepaying most effective?** Early in the loan, when most of each payment goes to interest.

**Does my lender recast automatically?** Usually not: extra payments shorten the term by default, and a recast may need a request and a fee.

**Are taxes, insurance or PMI included?** No — only loan interest.
