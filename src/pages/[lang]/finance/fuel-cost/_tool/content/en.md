## How to use

Enter the distance, your car's fuel economy and the price of gas. You'll see how much fuel the trip takes and what it costs — and, if you enter the number of people in the car, how much each person should chip in.

Fuel economy can be in mpg (US or UK), L/100 km or km/L. With US mpg the price is per gallon; with the other units, per liter.

## Formula

`gallons = miles / mpg`
`cost = gallons × price per gallon`

Example: a 300-mile trip in a car that gets 28 mpg with gas at $3.50 a gallon — about 10.7 gallons, **$37.50**. Split three ways, that's $12.50 each.

## FAQ

**Which mpg should I use?** Your real-world number rather than the sticker rating: highway driving usually beats it, while city traffic and a loaded car do worse. For a round trip, double the distance.

**How do I convert between mpg and L/100 km?** Just pick the unit you have, or use the [fuel economy converter](/en/convert/fuel-consumption/).

**Does it include tolls or wear and tear?** No, only fuel. Add tolls and parking separately. The IRS mileage rate, by contrast, covers the full cost of running a car, so it's much higher than fuel alone.
