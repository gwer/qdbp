## How to use

Enter the appliance's power in watts, how many hours a day it runs and your price per kilowatt-hour. You'll see how much energy it uses and what it costs per day, month and year.

The wattage is on the appliance's label or in its manual. If it's given in kilowatts, multiply by 1,000: 1.5 kW = 1,500 W.

## Formula

`kWh = watts × hours / 1000`
`cost = kWh × price per kWh`

Example: a 2,000 W space heater running 3 hours a day uses 6 kWh. At 17¢ per kWh that's $1.02 a day, **$30.60 a month**.

| TV | 60–150 W |

| Central AC | 2,000–4,000 W |
| Clothes dryer | 2,000–5,000 W |
| Space heater | 750–1,500 W |

## FAQ

**How do I enter power for a fridge?** If you use a 24-hour average power, enter 24 hours per day: the off periods are already included in that average. If you use the power while the compressor is running, enter only its actual running time. Do not apply both adjustments.

**Where do I find my rate?** On your electric bill: divide the total by the kWh used to get the all-in rate, including delivery charges. The U.S. average is around 17¢ per kWh, but it varies widely by state.
