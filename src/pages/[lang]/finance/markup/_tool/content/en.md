## How to use

Enter the cost and the markup percentage. You'll see the selling price, the profit per unit and the profit margin that markup gives you.

If you know the selling price and the markup and need the cost (say, to check a purchase price), choose “Cost from selling price” in the “Find” field.

## Formula

`selling price = cost × (1 + markup / 100)`

Cost $800, markup 25%: price **$1,000**, profit $200, margin **20%**.

## Markup vs. margin

Both express profit as a percentage, but of different bases:

- **markup** is profit as a percentage of **cost**;
- **margin** is profit as a percentage of the **selling price**.

So markup is always higher than margin:

| Markup | Margin |
|---|---|
| 25% | 20% |
| 50% | 33.33% |
| 100% | 50% |

A margin can never reach 100%; a markup can.

## FAQ

**How do I find the markup from a known price?** Divide profit by cost: (1,000 − 800) / 800 × 100 = 25%. The [margin calculator](/en/finance/margin/) shows both margin and markup.

**Is VAT or sales tax included?** No. Add it separately with the [VAT calculator](/en/finance/vat/) if your prices must include tax.
