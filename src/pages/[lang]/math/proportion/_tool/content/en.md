## How to use

Fill in the three numbers you know: if **A** corresponds to **B**, what does **C** correspond to? The calculator finds the unknown **X** and shows the formula with your numbers plugged in. This is also known as the rule of three.

## Direct and inverse proportions

**Direct proportion** — the more of one quantity, the more of the other: more items cost more, more servings need more flour.

`X = B × C / A`

**Inverse proportion** — the more of one quantity, the less of the other: more workers need fewer days, a higher speed means less travel time.

`X = A × B / C`

## Examples

- 3 lb of apples cost $6. How much do 5 lb cost? Direct: 6 × 5 / 3 = **$10**.
- A recipe for 4 servings uses 300 g of flour. How much for 6 servings? 300 × 6 / 4 = **450 g**.
- 4 workers finish a job in 12 days. How long will 6 workers take? Inverse: 4 × 12 / 6 = **8 days**.
- At 60 mph a trip takes 3 hours. How long at 90 mph? Inverse: 60 × 3 / 90 = **2 hours**.

## FAQ

**How do I know if it's direct or inverse?** Ask yourself: if the first quantity doubles, does the second one double too (direct) or halve (inverse)?

**Can I use a proportion for percentages?** Yes: 30 is to 150 as X is to 100, so X = 20%. The [percent of total calculator](/en/math/what-percent/) does this directly.
