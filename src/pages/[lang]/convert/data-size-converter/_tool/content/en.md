## How to use

Enter a data size and pick the units — the result and a table in every unit appear instantly.

## Decimal vs. binary units

- **Decimal** (SI): 1 KB = 1,000 bytes, 1 MB = 1,000 KB, 1 GB = 1,000 MB. Drive and USB stick capacities use these.
- **Binary** (IEC): 1 KiB = 1,024 bytes, 1 MiB = 1,024 KiB, 1 GiB = 1,024 MiB. Operating systems often show these — sometimes still labeled "GB".
- 1 byte = 8 bits.

## Examples

- A 1 TB drive = **931.3 GiB**, which is why Windows shows "931 GB".
- A 256 GB phone = **238.4 GiB**.
- A 100 Mbps connection = **12.5 MB/s**.

## FAQ

**Where did the missing space on my new drive go?** Nowhere — manufacturers count in decimal gigabytes, while your OS counts in binary ones. The gap is about 7% for GB and 9% for TB.

**Are megabits and megabytes the same?** No: a byte has 8 bits, so divide Mbps by 8 to get MB/s. Or skip the math: pick “Megabits (Mb)” in the list.
